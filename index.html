New Testament Prophets

October 17–23 • The same Holy Spirit who inspired Old Testament prophets continues in the New Testament, always centering Jesus Christ—His first coming, ministry, death, resurrection, and second coming. Prophetic words correct, sanctify, and protect us from unholy living while giving enough clarity about what is happening and what is coming so our faith is not shaken. Jesus is both the example and the power. We can only live this way as we abide in Him and let the Holy Spirit work on, in, and through us.

⚖️ Old Testament vs New Testament Prophets

Aspect Old Testament New Testament
Source Holy Spirit (same) Holy Spirit (same) — 1 Cor 12:4-6
Central Focus Coming Messiah, covenant with Israel Jesus Himself — first & second coming (Luke 24:44-47; Rev 22:6)
Primary Role Foretell, warn nation, call to repentance Edify, exhort, comfort the church (1 Cor 14:3; Eph 4:11-13)

👥 Prophets of the Old & New Testaments

Both lists are drawn from people Scripture explicitly calls prophets or describes as prophesying. Notice the shift: Old Testament prophets mostly called a nation back to covenant faithfulness and pointed forward to the Messiah; New Testament prophets announce that He has come, build up His church, and point to His return.

New Testament Prophets

Prophet Key Verses Key Message
Jesus Christ — The Prophet Deut. 18:15; Acts 3:22-23; John 6:14; Luke 24:19; Heb. 1:1-3 God's final and fullest Word. Every earlier prophet pointed to Him; He is both the Prophet and the subject of all prophecy. “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand” (Matt. 4:17).
John the Baptist Luke 1:76; Matt. 11:9-11; John 1:29; Mark 1:1-8 Prepare the way of the Lord; repent and be baptized; behold the Lamb of God. Jesus called him more than a prophet — the forerunner of the Messiah.
Zacharias (Zechariah) Luke 1:67-79 Filled with the Spirit, he prophesied that God had visited His people and raised up a horn of salvation in David's house — the covenant promises are being kept.
Simeon Luke 2:25-35 The infant Jesus is God's salvation, a light to the Gentiles and glory to Israel — yet also a sign that will be opposed, with a sword piercing Mary's soul (the cross foreseen).
Anna the prophetess Luke 2:36-38 An aged widow of constant prayer and fasting who gave thanks and spoke of the Child to all who looked for redemption in Jerusalem — witness through worship.
Agabus Acts 11:27-30; Acts 21:10-11 Foretold a great famine (moving the church to send relief) and Paul's arrest in Jerusalem. Predictive prophecy that prompted practical care and prepared believers for trial.
Prophets at Antioch Acts 13:1-3 Barnabas, Simeon Niger, Lucius, Manaen, and Saul. While worshiping and fasting, the Spirit directed them to set apart Barnabas and Saul — prophecy guiding mission.
Judas (Barsabas) & Silas Acts 15:22-32 Carried the Jerusalem Council's decision and, as prophets, exhorted and strengthened the Gentile believers with many words — edification and comfort (1 Cor. 14:3) in action.
Philip's four daughters Acts 21:8-9; Joel 2:28; Acts 2:17-18 Virgins who prophesied in Caesarea — living proof of Joel's promise that the Spirit would be poured out on sons and daughters. Their words are not recorded (non-canonical), yet their gift was genuine.
Paul Acts 13:1; 1 Cor. 14:37; 2 Thess. 2:1-12; 1 Thess. 4:13-18 Apostle and prophet. Christ crucified and risen, justification by faith, the coming apostasy, and the resurrection at the Lord's return. Insisted his words were the commandments of the Lord.
John the Revelator Rev. 1:1-3; Rev. 19:10; Rev. 22:6-10 The Revelation of Jesus Christ: the risen Lord walks among His churches, history ends in His victory, and “Surely I am coming quickly” (Rev. 22:20). Called by the angel a fellow servant with the prophets.
“The Bible is its own expositor. Scripture is to be compared with scripture.” — Ellen G. White, Education, p. 190

Old Testament Prophets (selected)

Prophet Key Verses Key Message
Moses Deut. 18:15-18; Deut. 34:10; Exod. 3; Num. 12:6-8 Deliverance from Egypt, the covenant and law at Sinai, and the promise of a coming Prophet like himself. God spoke with him face to face.
Samuel 1 Sam. 3:19-21; 1 Sam. 15:22-23; Acts 3:24 Obedience is better than sacrifice. Established the kingdom, anointed Saul and David, and headed the line of prophets who foretold Christ's days.
Nathan 2 Sam. 7:12-16; 2 Sam. 12:1-14 The everlasting Davidic covenant (fulfilled in Jesus), and fearless rebuke of the king's sin — “You are the man.”
Deborah Judges 4:4-9; Judges 5 Prophetess and judge who called Barak to trust God's word for victory; her song praises the Lord who fights for His people.
Elijah 1 Kings 17–19; 1 Kings 18:21; Mal. 4:5-6; Matt. 17:1-13 Stop wavering between two opinions — the Lord, He is God. Confronted Baal worship; his spirit returns in John the Baptist and the final-day message.
Elisha 2 Kings 2:9-15; 2 Kings 5; 2 Kings 6:16-17 A double portion of the Spirit; miracles of mercy for widows, lepers, and foreigners (Naaman). Those with us are more than those with them.
Isaiah Isa. 6; Isa. 7:14; Isa. 9:6-7; Isa. 53; Isa. 11; Isa. 65:17 The Holy God, the virgin-born Immanuel, the Suffering Servant wounded for our transgressions, the Branch of Jesse, and new heavens and a new earth.
Jeremiah Jer. 1:4-10; Jer. 23:5-6; Jer. 29:11-13; Jer. 31:31-34 Judgment and exile for covenant breaking, yet a new covenant written on the heart and a righteous Branch — “The LORD Our Righteousness.”
Ezekiel Ezek. 1–3; Ezek. 33:11; Ezek. 36:26-27; Ezek. 37 God takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked — turn and live. A new heart and God's Spirit within; dry bones brought to life.
Daniel Dan. 2; Dan. 7:13-14; Dan. 8:14; Dan. 9:24-27; Matt. 24:15 God rules over the rise and fall of empires. The Son of Man receives an everlasting kingdom; the Messiah's coming is timed; the sanctuary is cleansed. Jesus told us to read him.
Hosea Hos. 1–3; Hos. 6:6; Hos. 11:1-4; Hos. 14:1-4 God's faithful love for an unfaithful people, acted out in the prophet's own marriage. He desires mercy, not sacrifice; return and He will heal.
Amos Amos 3:7; Amos 5:21-24; Amos 9:11-15 God reveals His secrets to His prophets. Religion without justice is rejected; let justice roll down like waters. David's fallen tent will be raised.
Jonah Jonah 1–4; Jonah 4:11; Matt. 12:39-41 God's mercy reaches even Nineveh; repentance averts judgment. Jesus made Jonah's three days a sign of His own death and resurrection.
Micah Mic. 5:2; Mic. 6:8; Mic. 7:18-19 The Ruler from Bethlehem whose goings forth are from everlasting. Do justly, love mercy, walk humbly. God casts our sins into the depths of the sea.
Huldah 2 Kings 22:14-20; 2 Chron. 34:22-28 Prophetess consulted by King Josiah when the Book of the Law was found; she confirmed God's word of judgment and promised peace to the humble king — sparking national reform.
Malachi Mal. 3:1-3; Mal. 3:8-10; Mal. 4:1-6 The Lord will suddenly come to His temple, preceded by a messenger. Return to Me and I will return to you. The last OT word points to Elijah and the great day of the Lord.
“The Holy Scriptures are to be accepted as an authoritative, infallible revelation of His will.” — Ellen G. White, The Great Controversy, p. vii

What Stays the Same — and What Changes

Compare Old Testament Prophets New Testament Prophets
Direction of hope Looking forward to a Messiah who would come (Isa. 53; Mic. 5:2; Dan. 9:25) Announcing He has come, and looking forward to His return (Luke 2:30; Acts 1:11; Rev. 22:20)
Typical message “Return to the Lord” — covenant rebuke, judgment, restoration (Jer. 3:12; Hos. 14:1; Mal. 3:7) “Be built up in Christ” — edification, exhortation, comfort (1 Cor. 14:3; Acts 15:32)
Audience Kings and the nation of Israel/Judah; sometimes surrounding nations (Jonah, Obadiah) Local churches and the worldwide body of Christ, Jew and Gentile (Eph. 2:19-20; Acts 13:1)
Who prophesied Mostly a few called individuals; women such as Miriam, Deborah, and Huldah included Spirit poured out on all flesh — sons and daughters, young and old (Joel 2:28; Acts 2:17; Acts 21:9)
Canonical writings Many wrote Scripture (Isaiah, Jeremiah, the Twelve, etc.) A few wrote Scripture (Paul, John); many did not (Agabus, Anna, Philip's daughters) — their gift was still real
Test of a true prophet Fulfilled predictions and loyalty to the Lord (Deut. 13:1-5; Deut. 18:20-22; Isa. 8:20) Same tests, plus: confesses Jesus come in the flesh, good fruit, agreement with apostolic teaching (1 John 4:1-3; Matt. 7:15-20; Gal. 1:8)
Unchanging Same Holy Spirit (2 Pet. 1:21; 1 Cor. 12:4-6) • Same Jesus at the center (Rev. 19:10; Luke 24:27) • Same goal: a holy people ready to meet their God (Amos 4:12; 1 Thess. 5:23)

✝️ Jesus & His Comings Are Central to All Prophecy

Both the first and second comings of Jesus form the heart of biblical prophecy from Genesis to Revelation.

First Coming

  • Fulfillment of Messianic prophecies (Isaiah 53; Micah 5:2)
  • Luke 24:25-27, 44-47 — Jesus explained Himself from all the prophets
  • John 5:39 — Scriptures testify of Him
  • Hebrews 1:1-3 — God spoke through His Son
“The sacrifice of Christ as an atonement for sin is the great truth around which all other truths cluster.” — Ellen G. White

Second Coming

  • OT prophets foresaw ultimate restoration (Isaiah 11; Daniel 7)
  • NT points to His return (Matt 24; 1 Thess 4; Rev 19)
  • Revelation 22:6 — Lord God of the holy prophets
  • Until the Morning Star rises (2 Pet 1:19)
“Christ crucified, Christ risen, Christ ascended into the heavens, Christ coming again, should so soften, gladden, and fill the mind…” — Ellen G. White, Evangelism, p. 185
